# The Three Modes of Work

Context: [PURPOSE.md](PURPOSE.md). Everything I do day-to-day in Ultimate Brain falls into
one of three distinct modes. They're separated deliberately — Hermes needs to know which
mode a moment belongs to, because the whole point of Hermes is to stop Execution from
silently turning into Planning mid-stream.

## 1. Execution

Doing the work that's already been decided. Two sub-activities:

**Quick capture + processing**
Throughout the day, capture whatever comes up (a task, an idea, a thing to look into)
into the inbox without deciding anything about it yet. Separately, process the inbox —
route each item to where it belongs (a project, a due date, someday, delegated,
archived). Capture is zero-friction and constant; processing is a deliberate, periodic
pass.

**Setting up tasks for the day/week**
Choosing, from everything already captured and already prioritized, what actually gets
worked on today (My Day) and this week (My Week). This is small-scope, operational
sequencing — not deciding *what matters*, only deciding *what's in scope right now*. It's
grouped under Execution, not Planning, because it works off decisions that were already
made upstream (in Planning) — it doesn't make new ones.

Everything under Execution should feel mechanical once Planning has done its job: capture
it, file it, work it, mark it done. No re-deciding what the work should be.

## 2. Planning

Deciding what the work should be. Project planning, goal planning, setting priorities —
what I'm actually going to focus on — and open-ended brainstorming/discussion sessions.
This is where scope, direction, and tradeoffs get worked out, and where a plan gets
written down and (per [PURPOSE.md](PURPOSE.md)) locked until the next checkpoint.

This is also the mode most exposed to the failure pattern this whole project exists to
fix: perfectionism has unlimited runway here, because there's no natural stopping
condition other than "it feels done." Session structure (time-boxing, the 70% rule,
checkpoints) belongs here specifically.

**Area backlogs as a planning input.** Every Tag/Area has an Ongoing project (per the UB
docs' own recommended pattern for associating tasks with an Area). That Ongoing project's
task list functions as the backlog for that area of life — a holding pen of things that
matter to the area but haven't been promoted into a defined Project/Goal yet. A Planning
session can start by picking an item out of an Area's backlog and discussing whether it
deserves to become a Project, a Goal, a Note, or should just stay a task in the Ongoing
project as-is. See [planning-mode.md](planning-mode.md) for the full flow.

## 3. Review

Looking back at what happened and why — distinct from both of the above. Not doing the
plan, not making a new one: assessing whether the plan held, what actually got done vs.
committed to, and *why* any gap happened. Grounded in Ultimate Brain's own documented
rituals: My Day's Wrap-Up, My Week's "Reflect and Set Intent" (journal + meeting review),
and the quarterly/yearly goal check.

Review is the mode that makes the whole system trustworthy over time. It's where a
mid-week replan attempt that got logged (per PURPOSE.md) actually gets looked at: was
that a genuine blocker, or the perfectionism loop finding a new angle? Without a real
Review mode, that log is just data no one reads. This is deliberately kept separate from
Planning so that reflection doesn't quietly turn into another planning session — Review
asks "what happened," Planning asks "what next."

## Why the boundary matters

The core risk this whole project is designed against is Execution collapsing into
Planning without anyone noticing — "let me just optimize this one thing" mid-task. Naming
Review as its own mode closes the other leak: Review collapsing into Planning ("while I'm
reflecting on the week, let me also redesign next week") is the same failure pattern
wearing a different hat. Hermes's job is to recognize which mode a given moment is
supposed to be in and hold that boundary — not just between Planning and Execution, but
between all three.
